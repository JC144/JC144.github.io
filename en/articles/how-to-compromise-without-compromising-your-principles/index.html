How to Compromise Without Compromising Your Principles

The most important part of our job is knowing how to say no.

Compromise without compromising your principles. That line from Sébastien Lecornu must have struck a chord with a lot of product managers and product owners.

The most important part of our job is knowing how to say no. Every request on a project comes through us, whether it comes from decision-makers, other stakeholders, the technical team or our own managers. It’s our job to keep the product coherent, take responsibility for its features and stick to a schedule. Saying no keeps things moving.

Now we’ve got a new team member who can step into every role. AI makes it fairly easy for anyone to do just about anything.

Yes, we can add that button here, and here, and over there.
Yes, we can share user data.
Yes, we can show a pop-up on every page.

But AI doesn’t take into account the experience we want people to have or the technical and legal constraints we work under. It doesn’t share the vision that gives the product its character. That’s where people make a difference.

A product manager’s job is to understand the business need and work out where the value lies. From there, we can organise the work and make each person’s role clear. Our clients have their own jobs to do. They come to us because they need a tool and they’ve hired us to design a technical solution.

That’s where saying no comes in.

  • No, we’re not going to rebuild Excel. We’re here to solve a specific problem.
  • No, that feature isn’t a priority. Your teams rarely use it.
  • No, we’re not going to migrate your company’s entire history. Active projects come first.

Saying no is part of a longer conversation. Having a process gives us a way to get there. We can look into the request and assess the need behind it. If the idea came from AI, we can question its assumptions and explore it from other angles. We can talk to everyone involved to get a clearer picture of what the change would mean. Then we can propose where it fits on the roadmap, given the team’s capacity and its other priorities.

Say someone in sales asks for a pop-up. We’d start by asking what they want it to achieve. We’d look at the technical constraints with the teams involved and estimate the long-term risks and costs. Then we’d decide where the pop-up belongs on the roadmap, given what the team can take on and what else needs doing.

AI can make that work easier, but it has to work within limits we set in advance and maintain over time.

As Steve Jobs put it,

"People think focus means saying yes to the thing you've got to focus on. But that's not what it means at all. It means saying no to the hundred other good ideas that there are. You have to pick carefully. I'm actually as proud of the things we haven't done as the things I have done. Innovation is saying no to 1,000 things."
